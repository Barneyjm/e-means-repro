Interactive demo

Lloyd and e-means, side by side

The same points and the same starting centres, run through both algorithms. The clock is the number of passes over the data, so both have seen the same number of points at every moment.

Lloyd's k-means

  • Assign every point to its nearest centre, then move each centre to the mean of its points.
  • One update per pass over the data. The centres do not move until the pass is complete.
  • A centre that ends up with no points stays where it is.

e-means

  • Take a batch of B points. Each cluster keeps a decayed count N and sum S, both moved a step α toward the batch's count and sum. The centre is S/N.
  • α falls along a cosine from α0 to α0/10 over the run.
  • Every 5 updates, a cluster whose N is under 10% of the average takes half of a busy cluster, picked in proportion to N (orange rings).
  • After the last pass, each centre becomes the exact mean of the points it won during that pass.

What to look for

  • Lloyd's centres sit still for a whole pass and then jump. e-means' centres move after every batch, with large steps at first and small ones at the end.
  • After 3 passes, compare the two error curves at the same point on the x-axis. Then let Lloyd run on.
  • Turn revival off and use the tight clumps with a small k to see centres that get stranded between clumps.

Measured with this page's code over 30 random starts per setting (3,000 points, formulas on): after 3 passes, Lloyd's error is 13–50% above where it ends after 25 passes. e-means after 3 passes is within about 4% of Lloyd after 25, either way. It is better on the clumps and the uneven blobs at k = 8 and 20 (by 3–11% on average), and 0–4% worse at k = 48 and on the uniform square. Revival accounts for most of the gain on the clumps.

This is a 2-D toy. The paper's batch and step formulas were fitted on large high-dimensional sets, and the authors' code uses an eighth of that batch size for 16 dimensions or fewer and switches revival off in 2-D. This page keeps both on so they can be seen. The update rules are the same as in emeans_sklearn.EMeans; the code is demo-core.js.
