Follow-up experiments

The recipe on other algorithms

The e-means recipe is: update after every batch, keep forgetting running totals, a step that starts large and ends small, re-seed what dies, clean up from the last pass, settings from formulas. I tried it on other classic algorithms. The mixture model is animated below; the rest are charts of the measured results.

Gaussian mixtures, side by side

Ordinary EM computes each point's share in every component, sums over all points, and rebuilds the model once per pass. The online version does the same on a batch and moves running totals of weight, sum and sum of squares a step toward the batch's, with the same step curve, revival and clean-up as e-means. Ellipses are each component at two standard deviations (solid) and one (dashed); a thicker line means more weight.

Measured with this page's code over 20 random starts per setting (3,000 points; 6, 12 and 24 components; three data sets): ordinary EM needs a median of 15–29 passes to reach the log-likelihood of 3 online passes, and in 6 of the 180 starts it did not get there within 200. Both start from the same random points, with equal weights and the overall variance; the update rules are those of OnlineGaussianMixture (gmm-core.js).

Other algorithms

In every chart the dark yellow line is the usual full-pass method and the blue lines are the recipe. A dashed blue line uses the paper's formulas as published where they did not fit. Other colours are other baselines.

The pattern: the recipe pays where the full-pass method needs many passes (k-means, mixtures, hidden Markov models, NMF), pays little where it already converges in about ten passes (LDA, ALS), and has nothing to average where there is no noise (PageRank). The formulas were fitted on k-means; outside it, batch size and step had to be chosen again in every case except NMF and mixtures. Details and caveats: full write-up.
